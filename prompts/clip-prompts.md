# Individual Clip Prompts

Generate each clip separately. For each generation, combine the global direction in `master-visual-prompt.md` with the matching prompt below. Target approximately six seconds per clip. Keep the same reference images and character descriptions throughout.

## 01 — The First Morning (00:00–00:06)

An immense aerial view over the deserts of Sistan at dawn. Wind moves across empty streets in a small town beneath distant mountains. A lone fictional adult woman, Darya Mehran, stands on a rooftop holding a small radio. Begin extremely wide, then descend slowly until the human figure becomes visible. Dusty gold, restrained shadows, profound quiet, realistic atmosphere. Avoid readable signs or logos. Leave room for the opening voiceover.

## 02 — The Names (00:06–00:12)

Inside a damaged but recognizable school classroom, Sahar, a fictional adult teacher from Sistan, reads a handwritten list. Children's drawings flutter in a draft. A small radio glows on a desk, carrying indistinct reports. Compose intimate close-ups of fingers, paper, and Sahar's eyes, then slowly pull back to show the empty classroom. Emotion is restrained grief, not melodrama. No legible names or fabricated casualty data.

## 03 — A Country Awakens (00:12–00:18)

A visually spectacular but geographically grounded montage: Sistan desert, Kurdistan mountain ridges, Khuzestan industrial structures, Tehran skyline, and Hormuz's blue coastline. Fictional civilians gather in public squares. Use sweeping aerial transitions, immense scale, atmospheric depth, and natural environmental details. The music is to be added separately. No political banners, slogans, party marks, or readable text.

## 04 — The Crowd (00:18–00:24)

A huge fictional nighttime gathering in a contemporary Tehran street. Thousands of distinct civilians stand beneath the city lights. Darya, the same 32-year-old photojournalist from clip 01, records the gathering using her compact camera. Far down the street, a line of unmarked armored vehicles appears through haze. Begin overhead, then cut to a close shot of her camera and determined expression. Realistic crowd behavior; no readable signs or real insignia.

## 05 — The Order (00:24–00:30)

Inside an unmarked fictional armored command vehicle, General Kaveh Radan, a fictional 55-year-old commander with greying hair, watches the distant crowd through reinforced glass. Red instrument light shapes his controlled expression. Through the window, indistinct vehicle silhouettes advance through haze. Slowly push towards his face, then cut to the immense column. The character should appear severe but human, not cartoonish. Add the line “Advance. Clear the street” in post-production or as controlled dialogue.

## 06 — The Choice (00:30–00:36)

Nima Soroush, the same fictional 24-year-old conscript, stands inside the vehicle or near its open side, visibly conflicted. In the distance he notices a frightened child and an injured civilian. Radio noise and a commander's voice pressure him. Frame a tense close-up of Nima's eyes, then a brief subjective view of the civilians. He says, “Sir. They're civilians.” Radio reply: “Do what you're ordered.” Avoid showing weapon handling detail or a tactical sequence.

## 07 — Enough (00:36–00:42)

A short, emotionally charged fictional turning point. Nima shouts “ENOUGH!” and a single brief, non-graphic shot is heard. Cut to General Radan dropping to one knee, wounded in the leg; do not show wound detail. The armored vehicle stops. Prioritize Nima's shock, the sudden stillness, and the moral consequence. Use a quick handheld jolt and brief slow motion, then near-total silence. Do not depict firearm mechanics, aiming instruction, gore, or celebratory violence.

## 08 — The Silence After (00:42–00:48)

The unmarked armored column has stopped. Fictional soldiers look at one another, civilians stand in stunned disbelief, and Nima is frozen by the irreversible consequence of his choice. Darya's camera continues recording from the crowd. A wide frame holds drifting dust and subdued street lighting; slowly push towards Nima. The mood is stunned and mournful, not victorious.

## 09 — From Sistan to Hormuz (00:48–00:54)

A rapid, visually coherent montage of fictional civic gatherings and mutual aid across Sistan, Tabriz, mountain communities, Khuzestan, Tehran, and Hormozgan's ports. People help the injured, carry supplies, reopen community buildings, and protect neighbors. Mix sweeping aerials with short intimate shots. Show regional variety without stereotypes. No real political emblems, legible slogans, or documentary/news presentation.

## 10 — The People Who Stayed (00:54–01:00)

Dr. Laleh Vaziri, a fictional 40-year-old emergency physician, works with volunteers in an improvised clinic inside a converted railway terminal. A civilian and a young soldier arrive for treatment. Laleh firmly insists that both receive care. Use intimate handheld cinematography, practical medical lighting, believable expressions, and non-graphic treatment details. Dialogue: “Here, we save lives. All lives.”

## 11 — The Old Order Falls (01:00–01:06)

Inside a vast hangar containing fictional unmarked military vehicles, several soldiers quietly place their weapons on the floor and step away. Cut to an empty command room with unanswered radios and flickering practical lights. Use a symmetrical wide shot and a slow dolly backwards. The emotional tone is uncertainty and exhaustion. No executions, victory celebrations, actual insignia, or readable unit markings.

## 12 — Who Takes Iran? (01:06–01:12)

A monumental but plausible civic assembly chamber at night. Empty seats, dim abstract screens, and a distant city beyond tall windows. Darya stands before a camera, exhausted but composed. Begin wide and move slowly into a close-up. An off-camera fictional reporter asks, “Who takes Iran now?” Hold a pause before Darya replies, “That is exactly what we must not get wrong.” No real government seals, actual political symbols, or legible news graphics.

## 13 — The Next Country (01:12–01:18)

A temporary civilian assembly meets in a damaged public hall. Fictional adult teachers, doctors, port workers, students, and regional delegates sit around one large table. No throne, military command platform, or single leader. A sweeping crane shot reveals the whole assembly, then close-ups show listening, disagreement, and cautious resolve. Sahar says, “No one person gets to own our future.” Mehrdad replies, “Then let every province have a voice.” Warmth returns gradually, not triumphantly.

## 14 — The Dawn (01:18–01:24)

Sunrise across the Persian Gulf near Hormuz. A vast but believable gathering faces the brightening horizon. Darya lowers her camera; Nima stands apart, uncertain of what happens next; Dr. Laleh embraces a rescued child. Rise slowly above the coastline into an immense aerial view. The mood is fragile, earned hope. Darya's final voiceover: “Nobody takes Iran. The people decide what comes next.” Leave clean negative space for an editor-added title. Do not generate lettering or logos.
